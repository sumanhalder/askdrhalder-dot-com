How to choose a homeopath: 7 questions worth asking.

How to choose By Dr. Suman Halder · 4 min read

Not all homeopathic practice is the same. Here are the seven questions I'd ask before booking a consultation — and what the answers reveal.

1. "How long is your first consultation?"

If the answer is anything under 30 minutes, walk away. A classical homeopath needs 45–90 minutes for a first case-taking. Anything less means they're not taking a constitutional case.

2. "Will you give me one remedy, or a combination?"

Classical homeopathy uses one remedy at a time. If a practitioner offers "complex" or combination remedies, they're practising a different — and far less effective — discipline.

3. "How do you choose the remedy?"

Good answer: from the totality of your symptoms — physical, mental, and emotional — across the full case-taking.

Bad answer: based on the diagnosis alone, or on a machine-based "remedy finder", or on aura/energy testing. These are not classical homeopathy.

4. "What is your training?"

Look for a recognised BHMS or MD (Hom.) from a registered institution, plus ongoing classical training under a senior practitioner. Avoid "certified" practitioners from weekend courses.

5. "How often will I need follow-ups?"

Classical care involves regular follow-ups — typically every 3–6 weeks during active treatment. If a practitioner hands you a remedy and disappears for six months, that's not constitutional prescribing.

6. "What happens if the remedy doesn't work?"

A good classical homeopath will re-analyse the case, consider alternative remedies, and discuss whether homeopathy is the right tool for your situation. They will not simply prescribe a stronger dose of the same remedy.

7. "Can you tell me about your outcomes?"

Any experienced homeopath should be able to talk about their success rate in conditions they commonly treat. Be wary of vague claims ("homeopathy cures everything") and equally wary of false modesty.

Red flags to watch for

  • Guaranteed cures, or claims of curing cancer, severe psychiatric illness, or genetic conditions.
  • Pressure to abandon your existing medical treatment.
  • Refusal to discuss prognosis or expected timeline.
  • Heavy reliance on expensive tests, machines, or supplements.
  • No clear case-taking — short appointments, prescription-by-symptom.

The bottom line

Homeopathy is a complete medical discipline — but only when practiced classically. The difference between a trained classical homeopath and a generic homeopathic prescriber is roughly the difference between a classical musician and someone who can play a few chords.

Choose carefully. Ask questions. And if your homeopath isn't taking your case seriously, neither is the remedy likely to.
