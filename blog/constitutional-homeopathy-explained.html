Constitutional homeopathy, explained in plain English.

Foundations By Dr. Suman Halder · 6 min read

When patients come to my clinic for the first time, the most common question isn't about a remedy or a diagnosis. It's simpler — and more important:

"What does 'constitutional' actually mean?"

This article is my plain-English answer.

The short version

Constitutional homeopathy treats the person who has the disease, not the disease the person has. Two patients with the same diagnosis often need different remedies, because the underlying pattern — how they experience the illness, what makes it worse, what their energy is like, how they sleep, what they crave — is different.

The "constitution" is the totality of those patterns. A constitutional remedy is the one remedy that matches the totality of you, at this point in your life.

How this differs from what you've seen before

If you've had homeopathic treatment that didn't work, it usually wasn't because homeopathy doesn't work — it was almost certainly because:

  • The remedy was chosen for the diagnosis ("the piles remedy") rather than for the person.
  • Multiple remedies were combined or alternated in a single prescription.
  • The case-taking was too brief to surface the characteristic symptoms.
  • Follow-up was too infrequent to adjust when the case shifted.

Classical constitutional prescribing addresses all four problems. It is closer to how homeopathy was practiced in its founding era — slower, more careful, and built around the individual.

What a first consultation looks like

Plan for 45 minutes. We don't spend it on the chief complaint alone. I'll ask about:

  • The full picture of your main symptom — when, how, what makes it better or worse.
  • Your sleep, digestion, appetite, and food cravings or aversions.
  • Your temperature preferences (warm? cold? weather-sensitive?).
  • Your energy pattern across the day.
  • Your emotional life, stress patterns, and how you react under pressure.
  • Past illnesses, family history, and significant life events.

None of these questions are arbitrary. Every answer narrows the field of possible remedies. By the end of the consultation, a well-trained classical homeopath will usually have 3–5 candidate remedies in mind — and one will emerge as the constitutional match.

What happens after the remedy is chosen

You receive a single remedy, usually in a 30C or 200C potency, taken once or twice in the days following the consultation. Then we wait.

The "wait" is the hard part for patients used to instant relief. Classical homeopathy works on the body over weeks, not hours. The first signs of response are often subtle — better sleep, more stable energy, less emotional reactivity. The chief complaint comes next, often with a temporary intensification (a healing aggravation) followed by clear improvement.

When constitutional prescribing isn't right

I'd be lying if I said it was the answer to everything. Constitutional care works beautifully for:

  • Chronic, long-standing conditions.
  • Recurrent acute episodes (migraines, sinusitis, UTIs).
  • Conditions with strong mental and emotional components.
  • Patients willing to commit to months of follow-up, not days.

It works less well for acute emergencies, structural damage requiring surgery, or patients who want a quick fix rather than a deep cure.

The bottom line

Constitutional homeopathy is the most demanding form of homeopathy to practice — and the most rewarding for patients. It asks more of the doctor (time, attention, training) and more of the patient (patience, honesty, follow-through). When both sides commit to the process, the results can be profound and lasting.

If you've been told homeopathy doesn't work, you may simply have been seeing the wrong kind of homeopath. Classical constitutional care is a different discipline — and it's the one I practice.
